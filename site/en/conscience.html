How does consciousness arise from the brain?

Distant horizon

Why it matters

Knowing how consciousness arises would help doctors care for unresponsive patients, such as those in a coma, clarify which animals are conscious and what we owe them, and guide how society handles AI systems that seem conscious.

What we know

Brain areas and rhythms associated with conscious experience have been identified.

What blocks us

We don't know why physical activity produces experience, and we have no objective measure of consciousness.

Current leads

Rival teams test their theories (integrated information, global workspace) together in shared experiments.

In depth

Where things stand

A major 2022 review describes four prominent families of theories: higher-order theories, global workspace theories, re-entry and predictive processing theories, and integrated information theory 1. Its authors note that it is unclear how these theories relate to each other, or whether experiments can tell them apart 1.

Competing ideas

Global neuronal workspace theory links conscious experience to a sudden, widespread ignition of brain activity involving the prefrontal cortex 2. Integrated information theory instead links it to sustained, integrated activity in the back of the cortex 2.

What would settle it

The review's authors call for a mature practice of testing theories against each other 1. In an adversarial collaboration, supporters of rival theories jointly design experiments that test the predictions of each one 2.

Latest developments

In 2025, such a test, involving 256 participants and three methods (functional MRI, magnetoencephalography and recordings from electrodes inside the brain), was published in Nature 2. Its results challenged key claims of both theories: the sustained synchronization in the back of the cortex expected by integrated information theory was not found, and the ignition of activity expected by global workspace theory when a stimulus disappears was largely absent 2.
