How did human language emerge?

Distant horizon

Why it matters

Language is a distinctive trait of our species, so explaining how it arose is a key part of understanding how humans became who they are.

What we know

Several of the abilities it needs exist in other animals, but none combines them all.

What blocks us

Language leaves no fossils.

Current leads

Comparative genetics, animal communication studies, models of how languages evolve.

In depth

Where things stand

A 2014 review by eight researchers, including Noam Chomsky, concluded that the most fundamental questions about the origins of our capacity for language remain as mysterious as ever 1. Fossils and stone tools show the size of our ancestors' brains and their technology, but not the grammar or the meaning of anything they may have said 1.

Competing ideas

One debate concerns animals: the 2014 review argued that animal communication offers virtually no relevant parallels, since animal signals are largely innate and are not combined into new structures 1. The same review notes that no gene has yet been linked step by step to the capacity for language 1. FOXP2, first identified for its role in human speech, was thought to have been favoured by recent natural selection in our species, but a 2018 analysis of hundreds of genomes from around the world found no evidence of this 3.

What would settle it

The review's authors argue that models of language evolution have so far rested on unfounded assumptions and offered no empirical tests 1. In their view, what needs explaining is the underlying computation, including the ability to combine elements into new structures, which neither fossils nor artifacts reveal 1.

Latest developments

In 2025, a study of wild bonobos using methods from linguistics found that every call type in their repertoire appears in at least one compositional combination 2. Three combinations showed non-trivial compositionality, a property thought to be absent in animals, revealing stronger parallels with human language than previously thought 2.
